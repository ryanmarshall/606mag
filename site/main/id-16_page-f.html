You had been living together for seven years now. You were 28.

Sam broke a lot of ground over the years in getting his artwork shown in all the major American cities: New York, Chicago, Los Angeles. A gallery from London contacted him recently with interest in giving him a solo show. Years of frustrating struggles finally paid off, and, of course, he was indebted to you, to your benevolent support.

And he made you forget the day's labor, would erase its memory and ease your mounting anger that lingered. He always asked why you put yourself through it.

Your response was always the same: Because some days I know why I chose this. Some days it feels good.

Some days, he would murmur under his breath and return to whatever he was doing. You could feel the implication in the asperity of his whisper.

The years passed at lightning speed, year after year, until you found yourselves at 30. Thirty years, 11 years together. Is it right to keep on going like this? You were familiar with all the dating services that inundated your e-mail daily, the print ads on the CTA, the newspaper classifieds.
